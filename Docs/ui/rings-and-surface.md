# Rings and surface

The panel's surface (solid, light, Liquid Glass), what ring colour means, the arcs a ring can carry, and the animated marks. Input and hit testing: [input.md](input.md).

## Light panel

Optional, **off by default** (`AppSettings.usesLightPanel`, #74): the solid surface drawn light — `PanelLight.fill` (grey 0.97) with a `PanelLight.edge` hairline (black 0.12) so it keeps an edge on a white page — with dark content. It is a colour scheme, not a second set of views: `FloatingUsagePanelContent.panelScheme` pins `.light` instead of `.dark`, `PanelSurface` picks its fill from `\.colorScheme`, and content drawn in `.primary` follows.

- **The hairline is drawn inside the outline.** The window is exactly card + rail wide, so a line drawn outside is cut off at the window's edge. It strokes the *normalized* path (the card's tail is a separate, overlapping subpath) and leaves out `PanelSurface.screenEdge` — the docked side, or the notch berth's top — where it would be a grey line along the display's own edge.
- **Solid surface only, notch included.** Glass stays pinned dark (below); while glass is on the setting is kept but ignored, and its row is greyed out rather than hidden. At a notch the berth is light too: a surface that silently turned black when docked read as a bug.
- **The usage colours have light twins.** `pulseGood` / `pulseCaution` are about 1.5:1 on a light surface. Each `Color.pulse*` is a dynamic `NSColor` that resolves a deeper twin under an aqua appearance, ≥ 3:1 on `PanelLight.fill` (`UsageTintTests` pins the switch and the ratio). The menu uses the same twins where macOS draws it light. A colour chosen per account (`RingTint`) is the reader's and is not adjusted.

## Liquid Glass

Optional, **off by default** (`AppSettings.usesGlass`, `PanelSurface`); black is the default because the panel sits over the user's work all day. Measurements and history: [../decisions/liquid-glass.md](../decisions/liquid-glass.md).

- **Dim the glass, draw white on it.** With the active-appearance gate open, `.clear` glass is nearly transparent (white over a white page, black over a terminal). So it carries `PanelGlass.dim` — black up to 0.6, set by the **Transparency** slider (`AppSettings.glassTransparency`, default 0.5 → 0.3), passed down as `\.glassTransparency` — and the panel is pinned dark on **both** surfaces with `.environment(\.colorScheme, .dark)`, not `preferredColorScheme` (window-wide). Content still uses `.primary`, not hardcoded white. **Past the default the text follows the slider from white to black** (`PanelGlass.ink`: the label colour up to `inkTurns` 0.5, then `Color(white:)` at 0.85 opacity down to black at 1, applied as the root `.foregroundStyle`), and past `schemeTurns` (0.75) the panel's scheme turns light so hairlines, tracks and the usage colours follow (`PanelGlass.scheme`). The reader asked for this: white text vanished over a light page at the clear end. Black text vanishes over a dark window instead, and Pulse cannot see what is behind the panel without recording the screen; the slider's caption says so. `GlassInkTests`.
- **Liquid, not frosted, depends on `FloatingPanel` overriding the private `_hasActiveAppearanceIgnoringKeyFocus` to return `true`.** Glass in an inactive window is drawn as a flat blur, and this panel is always inactive. Do not make the panel key-eligible instead: that holds only until the app's first deactivation and puts focus at risk. If macOS stops asking the selector, the glass reverts to frosted and nothing else breaks.
- **`Glass.clear`, not `.regular`** (`.regular` measured, historically, as opaque milky white in a transparent panel). Needs macOS 26; older systems get a vibrant blur (`.ultraThinMaterial`).
- **Keep the surface in hit testing.** `allowsHitTesting(false)` is what stopped gap-dragging; the window takes the drag in `sendEvent` before any view sees the event ([input.md](input.md)).
- **Glass needs a pixel under it** (`PanelGlass.claim`, black 0.01). The system composites the glass and its tint, leaving the window's own pixels empty, and the window server hands a press on empty pixels to whatever is behind, so `sendEvent` never sees it (#68: a docked rail draggable only by its rings). Verified by real input after the fill went in. Do not remove it or take it to zero.

## Colour and spent

Colour means **usage**, not identity (`UsageTint`: green / amber / red / spent deep red). `Provider` carries no accent; the icon is the brand. Brand-coloured rings read as a warning (Claude's orange at 3% used).

**Warning threshold.** `AppSettings.warningThreshold` (`WarningThreshold`, 60–90%, default 75) is where amber becomes red, and it bounds that step only: green stays under `UsageTint.cautionThreshold` (0.5), below every offered figure, so the two steps cannot cross. It reaches the panel through `EnvironmentValues.usageWarningThreshold`, set once in `FloatingUsagePanelContent` — one number every ring, bar and figure must agree on, and `UsageRingView`'s initializer is already at the type-checker's budget. It is not a layout input, so it is not in `PanelMetrics`. `UsageTint.color(for:isExhausted:warningAt:)` and `UsageWindow.tint(warningAt:)` take it with **no default**: a default is how one ring comes to disagree with its neighbour.

**Per-account `RingTint`** is opt-in: system colour well, stored as hex, converted through **sRGB**, no opacity (translucent reads as "no reading"). Spent still uses the spent colour; `UsageRingView.isSpent` comes from the **provider**, not the fraction, because a lock can happen well short of 100%.

**Collapsed alert colour.** `FloatingUsagePanelContent.alertTint` finds the worst headline window across the rail and, past `warningThreshold` or exhausted, hands its `UsageTint` colour to `UsageDockView.alert`. An ordinary `PanelSurface` applies it only while collapsed (`isExpanded ? nil : alert`; expanded, the rings already say it). A physical notch has no collapsed surface, so `NotchAlertShape` draws the colour as a 2pt edge directly below the housing. `AppSettings.dockShowsAlertColor` (on by default) gates both inside `alertTint`, so off is the same `nil` an all-green rail produces. It exists because several accounts past the threshold otherwise leave a line coloured for as long as it is watched, which reads as a stuck pixel rather than a warning. [settings.md](settings.md)

**Countdown** (`showsRemaining`): the arc follows the figure; colour still means closeness to the limit. No reading → empty track either way; spent fills the ring. Do not invert `nil` into a full "100% left" circle. [../refresh-and-data.md](../refresh-and-data.md)

## Hover halo

The pointed-at halo hangs on the **progress arc**, not the ring view, and its inward half is **masked**. Not a `.shadow` on the composed view (it draws behind the icon and antialiased edges leak tint), not an opaque disc (invisible on black, a white coin on glass); a mask has no colour to get wrong. Measurements: [../decisions/liquid-glass.md](../decisions/liquid-glass.md).

## Window-clock arc

`showsWindowClock`, default off. Drawn **outside** the usage ring (inside belongs to the activity mark), neutral and low opacity, not a second hue. `windowClockDirection` chooses filling with elapsed time (the default, so existing installs do not reverse) or emptying with time remaining. Either direction needs both the provider-reported reset and duration (`reportsLength` must be true); with either missing it is nil. Applied as an overlay **after** `.frame(width: diameter…)`, never as a ZStack child (a wider child grew the usage ring). Own 60s ticker, not the usage loop.

## Second ring

`showsSecondRing`, default off. `ProviderUsage.secondWindow(preferring:)` returns **the fullest limit in the ring's own model group**, else the fullest of everything else. Nil where the provider reports one limit: an empty second ring reads as a limit at zero, or as a fault.

- **The group comes first because a provider can report independent budgets.** Antigravity reports a five-hour and a weekly for `Gemini` and the same pair for `Claude and GPT`; pairing a Gemini weekly with a Claude five-hour would put two unrelated pools on one mark. Claude Code's five-hour and weekly are unscoped, so they pair with each other and the model-scoped weekly stays on the card. "Fullest" rather than "the weekly one" needs no per-provider table and still works when the ring is pinned. `max(by:)` keeps the first of equals, so untouched windows stay in the provider's order between passes.
- **Inside, not outside.** Outside is the clock arc's, and the limit that matters most stays the outer, bigger, thicker ring, so nothing moves for anyone who leaves this off. Same colour language as the first ring; size and weight tell them apart. Spent fills it, whichever way it counts.
- **Geometry lives in `DockLayout.secondRing…`, not in `PanelMetrics`**: nothing outside the ring changes size. At standard scale the band between the icon disc and the ring's inner edge is 6pt and the activity mark rides its middle; with the second ring on, the mark moves in against the disc and the disc gives up 2pt a side. The ring, the rail's width and the ring centres are unchanged.
- **The percent label follows the outer limit only.** Two figures there would change `DockLayout.percentTextWidth`, a budget the whole rail is measured from.

## One ring per model group

`AppSettings.splitAccounts`, per account, default off, offered only where `Provider.splitsByModelGroup` is true — Antigravity alone, whose plan reports a Gemini allowance and a Claude-and-GPT one as two `scope`s of one login. One ring could only show the busier. Off by default because a second ring costs a slot, and docked, the rail is the whole panel.

- **The rail is slots, not accounts.** `RailSlot.rail(for:isSplit:groups:)` is the one place the order is decided; the panel's `entries` and `PanelHitArea.slot(at:)` both go through it, so a ring is never drawn at one position and refreshed from another. A slot is an account plus an optional group; `id` is `account@group`.
- **A limit never disappears into the split.** `modelGroups(of:)` is all-or-nothing: a reading that mixes scoped and unscoped windows yields no groups. More groups than `modelGroupCount` leaves the account whole rather than dropping the extra group — one ring showing the busiest of three is better than a limit nobody can see.
- **One slot until a reading carries more than one group**: one ring that becomes two beats two empty ones that may never fill. Groups keep the provider's reported order (what Antigravity's own screen shows).
- **Both rings are the same account**: icon, tint, key, refresh. Clicking either refreshes the login. They differ in windows (`windows.filter { $0.scope == group }`) and title, which appends the group so VoiceOver and the card do not repeat themselves.
- **The budget moves with it.** `AppSettings.railSlotCount` counts a split account as `provider.modelGroupCount` (a fixed 2, so the rail does not resize when a reading is one group short) and feeds `PanelMetrics.makeRoom(for:)`.

## What may not be animated

The rail's `railTop` / `railLeading` offsets track the pointer, so they sit **outside** `.animation(_:value:)`, applied after the card's reveal. Inside the card's `value: selectedSlot` spring, the first frame of a drag closed the card and sprang the rail's position too, so the panel slid away from the hand dragging it.

## Activity mark

White arc on the band between the icon disc and the usage stroke — **or just outside the disc while the second ring is on**. Core Animation, not `TimelineView`. Reset `spinning` on disappear. Not drawn on a ring showing an animated mark (below). [../refresh-and-data.md](../refresh-and-data.md)

**`AppSettings.animatesRingActivity`** (on by default) is the one switch over this arc and the refresh mark drawn over the usage arc during a fetch. `UsageRingView.isBusy` / `isRefreshing` track the facts either way; off only withholds the cue — no travelling arc, no refresh sweep, no dimming of the usage arc while it fetches. [settings.md](settings.md)

`AppSettings.botMarks`, **per account**, default off, switched on in that account's provider pane. It reaches the ring through `RailEntry.showsBotMark`, as the chosen tint does, not through the environment. A logo says which of seventy-seven products a ring is; a mark trades that for motion, worth it only on the rings somebody watches work. ### Personas and scenes

**Eight personas** (`BotMarkPersona`, `BotMarkChoreography`), each with its own idle signature, three-beat everyday scene, four-beat work scene, refresh and attention responses, and finish acknowledgement; `tempo`, `motionScale`, `gazeScale` and `eyeScale` accent those scenes. **A persona changes how a mood is expressed, never the reading**: task effects need work or fetching, a spent limit gets a downcast or alarmed response, a missing reading an uncertain one.

- **Automatic** (the default) deals personas **by position on the rail**, so neighbours differ; eight rings pass before one repeats. A per-account choice in the provider pane (`AppSettings.botPersonas`, absent = automatic) wins; a stored persona that no longer parses reads as automatic.
- **The body is the reader's, round by default** (`BotMarkBody`, `AppSettings.botShapes`, absent = `blob`). All eighteen upstream shapes are available whatever the persona; a scene may morph into an effect but never changes the resting silhouette.
- **A scene plays every beat in order.** `BotMarkRoutine` stores each state with its duration (its cached state/hold tables are shared immutable data); `BotMarkProgramme.forMood` selects the scene and requests sequential playback. The engine enters at the signature pose and completes the scene before looping, with a small random hold within each beat's bounds. Random selection skipped characteristic actions and made differently ordered lists identical; raw engine callers can still request it.

The arrows are the actual sequence. Idle beats generally last 1.2–6.5s (sleepy rests longer), work beats 1.8–6s; `BotMarkChoreography.swift` is authoritative for each hold. Every persona has a different **set** of states in every mood.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calm / 沉稳 | idle → listening → shy | working → thinking → searching → writing | receiving → listening | listening → idle | happy |
| Eager / 热切 | happy → curious → playful | excited → spawning → working → radar | spawning → radar → receiving | happy → curious | excited |
| Steady / 踏实 | humming → listening → idle | working → writing → searching → orbit | loading → orbit | listening → humming | humming |
| Curious / 好奇 | curious → suspicious → surprised | searching → radar → working → thinking | radar → searching → thinking | curious → listening | surprised |
| Sleepy / 迟缓 | shy → idle → happy | working → thinking → searching → receiving | listening → loading | shy → listening | shy |
| Playful / 顽皮 | playful → bouncing → laughing | excited → orbit → searching → writing | orbit → receiving | playful → surprised | laughing |
| Stoic / 冷面 | suspicious → listening → idle | searching → radar → working → loading | searching → thinking | suspicious → listening | proud |
| Proud / 得意 | proud → happy → laughing | excited → orbit → working → spawning | thinking → loading → radar | proud → happy | playful |

- **Everyday and working vocabularies are disjoint across the whole cast.** Humming, playful bouncing, curious/listening and proud/suspicious poses are everyday only; work alternates working/searching/excited faces with task morphs. An idle character therefore never looks like another persona's work; changing tempo alone did not distinguish mixed pools.
- **Spent and unavailable responses are authored per persona** (eager/startled and stoic/annoyed differ from calm/downcast and proud/embarrassed). None includes a carefree happy scene, a work effect or a reset celebration.
- **Only the sleepy persona (「迟缓」) may doze.** Its awake scene is `shy → idle → happy`, with slower tempo and smaller motion and gaze scales. It may add `drowsy` at night or in its spent/unavailable responses, alternating with sad/confused rather than settling into a sleeping face. No other persona receives drowsy, sleeping, powering-down or bored. This follows the resolved persona, chosen or dealt.
- **29 of the 39 catalogue states are reachable**, including orbiting dots, radar waves, loading ribbons, incoming dots, the playful bouncing ball and the spent-limit exclamation. Idle keeps the face most of the time; only playful briefly morphs into the ball. Sending/uploading/dictating/dragging/notification badges need their event, and the determinate `progress` fill would imply progress Pulse did not measure. Sleeping, waking, powering-down and bored are in no production scene.
- **Short scenes get their full eye vocabulary.** Returning to a state advances through its expression pool instead of restarting it; first entry keeps the characteristic expression. A 2s pose never reached the 9–16s expression timer, which left most of the artwork unreachable.

### Moods and events

**Five moods, and no sixth** (`BotMarkMood`), resolved from existing facts: busy → working scene, refreshing → refresh scene, spent → spent response, no reading → unavailable response, otherwise idle. Busy outranks the others. Local work detection comes from Claude Code and Codex transcripts; other providers get everyday scenes without pretending that a quota change proves work. **Nothing here reads the warning threshold**: the ring already says how close the limit is.

- **Pointer attention is personal, and interrupts idle only.** An idle mark switches to its persona's attention scene and follows the pointer; a working mark keeps working; a spent or unavailable one keeps its response rather than greeting the reader as if the account were fine.
- **`angry` joins the playlist out of hours and only then** (`BotMarkHours`, outside 09:00–21:00 on weekdays; weekends count as out of hours). The hours are a guess — the app has no calendar — kept in one file and outside the mood table so they cannot pass for a reading.
- **Night is separate from overtime.** `BotMarkHours.isNight` is **23:00–08:00 local time**, every day; a Saturday afternoon does not make idle marks drowsy.
- **Quiet only adds an accent to sleepy.** Twenty minutes with nothing written by the watched CLIs (`AgentActivityMonitor.lastWrite`; **nil is not quiet**) permits a `drowsy` beat at night for sleepy alone: its three awake beats total **14–19 seconds**, then **2–3 seconds** of drowsy, then the scene restarts awake. There is no daytime boredom accent, and CLI silence is not evidence that other providers are idle. `BotMarkRestTests` checks the restriction across every mood, quiet/hover condition, weekday/weekend and day/night ([../testing.md](../testing.md)). Historical probe (2026-09-19): with work's 2.5–4.5s holds and `isOvertime` as night, ordinary personas were drowsy about half of a quiet Saturday night, and reweighting alone was not enough.
- **Two one-shots** (`BotMarkEvent`), both facts Pulse witnessed. A limit turnover (`ResetWatch`, the same unambiguous rule as notifications) plays the full 6.4s `celebrate` — nine turns and ribbons. A turn that just ended (`AgentActivityMonitor.finishedAt`) plays **2.6 seconds** of the persona's acknowledgement; the monitor fences scans that outlive it, so stopping monitoring is not a fabricated finish. An event interrupts the scene, plays once and returns; it is not replayed while its fact holds, and a reset outranks a finish. **Its window must close on time** — 20s for a reset, 6s for a finish — because the engine re-arms only on nil; `FloatingUsagePanelView` looks again each second while an event shows. Left set, a remounted mark replayed it and the next real finish played nothing.
- **Ribbons are a busy/event cue, at every size** (including 25pt). `BotMarkProgramme.configuration` enables particles only for working, fetching, or a one-shot actually playing; idle and attention spins draw none. The engine marks the active event interval, so an event flag still true after its 2.6/6.4s must not re-enable them, and live particles and pending emissions are cleared when busy/event playback ends. The Reduce Motion master switch wins.

### Gaze and pointer

- **The eyes follow the real pointer.** `PanelPointerWatcher` samples a position (`.onHover` does not work on this window, [input.md](input.md)); it is handed down the rail and each mark measures it relative to itself through a `GeometryReader` in the panel's named coordinate space, divided by four radii (upstream: two) so the ring under the cursor looks hardest and its neighbours part of the way. Nil, when the pointer leaves the panel, returns each mark to its own wandering gaze.
- **The pointer outranks the mark's habits.** The expression's built-in glance reaches 76 units and the edge lean 7, against a pointer worth 22; on a right-docked rail they pulled a mark to stare away from the cursor. Upstream damps the state's glance to a fifth while the pointer is on the panel; the lean and the artwork's glance give way in the same measure — the artwork's by subtracting most, not all, of the eye pair's own offset, so a `sad` mark still looks sad. `BotMarkTests` checks every persona, facing the screen, with the pointer on the far side.
- **It is a look, not a switch** (`BotMarkEngine.attention`). Each mark notices after its own random 50–250ms, then a critically damped spring hands the gaze over in about 0.4s; leaving eases back with no delay. The largest one-frame eye step on arrival went from 30–60 units to 1–5. `BotMarkAttentionTests` holds it as the median of twenty-one runs, with playful left out (its own gestures dart the eyes as far).
- **The mark faces away from the screen edge** (`BotMarkGaze`): docked right looks left, docked left looks right, a top rail looks ahead. Two mechanisms, because neither works alone: a standing lean of seven units added to (not replacing) the wandering gaze, and *reflecting* the gaze for the left-facing case. An expression's eye pair is itself off-centre (from 44 units left of the head's centre to 76 right across the twenty-five), so turning also reflects the pair about the head, swapped left for right, blended point by point. Not reflected: the pointer (a real place on screen) and a `notifying` mark's glance at its badge (fixed on the body).
- **It turns the gaze, not the body** (`BotMarkEngine.facing`, spring frequency 9 / damping 0.85, about a third of a second, so a change of edge reads as a decision). Mirroring the whole draw transform flipped the body over like a card; `BotMarkTests` asserts the body's horizontal scale stays positive and it travels no further across a turn than at rest. The **first** frame snaps to its target, so marks do not swing on launch. The rail keeps its views across an edge change — `AnyLayout` and a `ForEach` keyed by account id, `NSHostingView` built once — which is what lets the spring see the change.
- **Measured, not eyeballed.** The lean alone (1.2.0) left a right-hand rail averaging right of centre for four of eight personas; its test had switched off the autonomous gaze. `BotMarkTests` now runs every persona in both moods for ten minutes each, everything on, and asserts the mean is left of centre.
- **No eye limit of Pulse's own.** An eye is bounded only by the upstream's clamp to the body's silhouette and may sit against the edge ([../decisions/bot-mark-geometry.md](../decisions/bot-mark-geometry.md#the-originals-motion-is-not-trimmed)).

### Motion

- **Working must look like working**, since the mark replaces the activity arc. Rotation cannot: its 5 rad/s spring low-passes a 1.6Hz signal (a 2.4× target moved the swing from 1.1° only to 2.6°). **Squash carries a pulse** (`squashScale`, ×3 for working; measured 3.7% peak-to-peak height against idle's 1.3%), **clamped to 0.9…1.12** — a guard, not tuning: ×10 read as the mark resizing, and the same multiplier collapsed `excited` to a fifth of its height. **Tempo halves** (`tempoEmphasis`): blinks and glances twice as often, and the spin every 3–4.5s instead of 6–9. The spin throws **ribbons** (measured on 158 of 720 frames over twelve seconds). Neither emphasis touches translation, which stays the upstream's.
- **A change of state never teleports the body** (`BotMarkEngine.render`: `drawn`, `carry…`). Celebrate's turns, a gesture's shake and a morph's pose are drawn directly, so a switch mid-motion jumped up to 144° or 108 units in one 30fps frame (ordinary motion peaks near 15). The difference between the last drawn frame and the new state's first is handed to critically damped springs (frequency 12) that ease it to zero; angles unwind the short way. The springs must be **built at the difference with a target of zero** — `BotMarkSpring(value)` targets its own value, which left the carry standing until the body left the canvas. `BotMarkContinuityTests` bounds the worst frame at 30 units / 30°.
- **The drawn position is not bounded**, as upstream: the viewBox leaves ~15 units around the body, `drowsy` nods 25 and a bounce throws the body 48, so the head can be clipped for a moment.
- **Reduce Motion** draws the scene's first signature pose as a settled still, not a frozen first frame, so the mood stays legible. It is solved **outside** the draw closure and memoised (a still is three seconds of simulated animation, and a draw happens on every pointer move); the key includes eye/gaze scales and canvas width as well as body and motion settings. It waits out a blink by asking whether one is *in flight*, not by an eyelid threshold: `drowsy` rests at 0.34 and never blinks.
- **30fps** (`TimelineView(.animation(minimumInterval:))`), not the display's rate: invisible at ring size and cheaper — settled, seven animated rings cost ~15% of a core against ~19% at display rate, over a ~10% baseline with none. `TimelineView` does tick on this panel (measured once on a real docked rail: three captures 0.45s apart all differed); that is one measurement on one machine.
- **Paused while the rail is collapsed** (`EnvironmentValues.botMarksPaused`, set by `UsageDockView` on its rings; `TimelineView(.animation(minimumInterval: 1/30, paused:))`). Collapsed, the rings stay in the tree at opacity 0 so nothing moves as the rail opens, and the timeline went on ticking behind the sliver: every mark redrawn 30 times a second and the whole panel laid out with them, for nobody. Measured on an M5, five marks, rail shut: 6–10% of a core before, under 2% after. The last frame stays drawn, so the fade-out is of what was there; the engine clamps a frame to 100ms as upstream does, so it resumes where it was. **Not a host per mark.** Tried 2026-10-14: each animated mark in its own `NSHostingView`, so a tick would not update the panel's. Unchanged on screen, but the process went from ~10% to ~22% of a core on an M5 with the rail out (five marks, twice each). Expanded, the busy main thread is SwiftUI's per-frame update and render; the mark's own maths is ~4% of it, so every extra host added a copy of the fixed cost rather than removing one. **Nor one display-link clock for all marks.** Tried the same day: a `CADisplayLink` at 30fps (`NSView.displayLink`) writing an `@Observable` time the marks read, after a small harness on the same M5 had a `TimelineView` laying the host out at the display's 120Hz and this cutting cycles by 35–45%. In Pulse the main thread's own samples fell, but the process did not: 5.47 / 8.60 / 14.07% against 5.50 / 7.50 / 9.27% for the timeline, alternating 30-second runs with the rail out. The harness's saving did not carry over; measure in Pulse, not in a harness.

### Size

`botScale` 1.4 against `iconScale` 0.8, measured against the **ring's inner edge**, not the disc: the canvas takes the full 28pt inside a standard 36pt ring, so the body is about 25pt with 1.6pt of clearance from the stroke. It needs that much: the 259-unit viewBox around a 229-unit body leaves margin for bob, lean and squash, and a face needs room for two eyes. It is wider than the 20pt disc on purpose (a near-black disc on a near-black rail reads as a shadow; the ring is the visible edge) but scales *with* it, so the 2pt a side the disc gives up to the second ring takes the mark too. Any bigger and the ring's stroke or its 4pt gap, both layout budgets, has to give.

### Colour

**Colour is identity, not status.** `BotMarkTint.brand` is the provider's own colour where it has one, lifted toward white below a luminance floor of 0.42 (several brands are black and would be a hole in the disc), with the eyes flipping to keep contrast. Kimi's is **`#7AA5FF`**, lighter than the former `#2F6BFF`, to keep its face bright at ring size. This is the one place brand colour is allowed on the panel; the ring beside it still means only how close the limit is (see the comment on `UsageWindow.tint(warningAt:)`).

- **Colourless providers are dealt a colour** by `BotMarkTint.deal(over:)`, given the rail's providers **in drawn order** (the dock does it; who sits next to whom is not knowable from `Provider.allCases`). The wheel is `BotMarkTint.paletteSize` = **17** hues, evenly spaced and levelled to one luminance (at one fixed lightness a yellow glares and a blue reads as black); 17 is prime, so every stride is coprime. The deal walks the wheel with a coprime stride; all 272 stride/rotation combinations are tried and the winner is the one whose closest *neighbouring* pair is furthest apart. Two brand colours side by side are not scored (MiniMax's two rows really are one red). The rail keeps its latest deal in a view-owned `BotMarkTint.DealCache`, keyed by ordered providers and chosen colours, so pointer, quota and activity updates reuse it.
- **No colour repeats below seventeen colourless accounts on one rail; past that the slot index wraps (`% paletteSize`) and a colour repeats** without any error. Reachable now: 61 of 78 cases are colourless (13 of 25 hand-written providers, 47 of 52 profiled ones — only five set [`ProviderProfile.brandColor`](../../Sources/Pulse/Providers/ProviderProfile.swift) — and `.pulseExtension`), and `BotMarkTests` checks distinctness only up to seventeen. The wheel was last widened, from eleven, when V2EX overflowed it. Whether anyone runs a rail that wide is unknown.
- **Rejected schemes**: ten hand-picked hexes (two greens 49° apart, blues 14°, ambers 13°); a hash of the provider id (outright collisions); a fixed palette in declaration order (a dealt amber beside Claude Code's clay); greedy choice (ran out of colours near the end of a long rail); hues only in the brands' gaps (crowded into yellow-green-to-purple at 24° apart).
- **A colour can be chosen per account** (`AppSettings.botColours`, absent = automatic), in the Automatic/Custom shape of the ring colour but separate from it: the ring means closeness to the limit, the mark means which provider. A chosen colour is drawn as picked, neighbours are dealt away from its hue as from a brand colour, and the luminance floor still lifts a black choice.
- **Inserting a provider shifts the dealt colours after it.** Nothing is stored against them, so an update only changes how the rail looks.
